Option-Only Portfolio Optimization with Greek-Induced Covariance and Conditional Risk Premia

Home . PDF . Code

Dhruv Kohli · July 2026

Developed a portfolio theory for books built entirely from listed options, where classical Markowitz breaks down: premium weights replace the unit budget, covariance is induced through a Greek/state factor map plus regularized residual risk, expected returns come from structural risk-premium channels instead of noisy historical contract means, and the allocation is a conic maximum-Sharpe program whose feasible set carries gross and short premium, stress-loss, margin, and pre-trade liquidity budgets inside the optimizer rather than as after-the-fact accounting.

Then took the theory to exchange-realistic practice: contract-level spread/fee/slippage/margin/assignment costs built from historical market-hours CBBO quotes, volume-aware participation caps, exact VRO/SOQ VIX settlement, and whole-contract execution. The headline book — a locked 56-name equity-option universe plus VIX options — earns a full-cost net Sharpe of 1.63 (2.01 gross) with net Sortino 4.38, beats both a stock-Markowitz baseline on the same underlyings and a capped naive option book (+1.34 Sharpe versus naive, p < 0.001), and holds up out of sample: rolling 36-month refits at 1.27 net Sharpe, positive purged combinatorial cross-validation in both test designs, Monte Carlo resampled and refit fifth percentiles above 1.0, and a deflated Sharpe ratio of 0.996 after charging for every candidate examined. The smaller eight-name VIX book confirms the mechanism at 1.38 net. Displayed option depth supports the book at $1M NAV and refuses it at $5M, making it a viable small-size book strategy.

Empirical Verdict

The paper tests four universes at a one-million-dollar account size: eight equity-option names, the same names plus VIX options, 56 names, and 56 names plus VIX options. The two VIX-enabled books pass the full-cost tests. The broad no-VIX book is mixed, and the original no-VIX book remains a capacity diagnostic rather than a deployable result.

The mechanism is narrow by design. Breadth only helps after replacing noisy contract-level historical means with structural forecasts, and option carry must be disciplined by stress, vega, margin, liquidity, and whole-contract constraints before the Sharpe ratios are treated as economic results.

Research paper only. The results are historical backtests, not investment advice, live trading performance, or evidence that the book can scale beyond the displayed-depth capacity checks.
