Option-Only Portfolio Optimization with Greek-Induced Covariance and Conditional Risk Premia

Home . PDF . Code

Dhruv Kohli · July 2026

Adapted Markowitz portfolio theory to books built entirely from listed options, where every position is a funded, expiring cashflow. Premium weights replace the unit budget, covariance is induced through a Greek factor map that keeps the factor and residual cross terms, expected returns come from structural risk-premium channels instead of noisy contract means, and the allocator maximizes cost-aware net utility under hard CVaR, stress, margin, collateral, and point-in-time liquidity gates. Cash is an admissible book, infeasible whole-contract conversions abstain, and zero wealth is an absorbing state that no later return can undo.

The headline model, the High Ceiling Allocator, runs a 93-month walk-forward on eight mega-cap equity option names plus VIX options from 2018 to 2026. Net of spreads, fees, slippage, borrow, and margin funding it earns 16.0 percent annualized with a 1.18 Sharpe ratio, a 2.28 Sortino ratio, and a 17.1 percent maximum drawdown. A licensed-quote execution audit then replays every frozen trade against 82 million OPRA quotes and reprices fills at observed spreads: the book holds a 2.05 Sortino paying the full displayed touch and 2.03 under worst-case entry spreads, with 94.1 percent of entry weight covered by real quotes. Validation includes purged combinatorial cross-validation, Monte Carlo resampling and refits, a 435-check verification suite, and a 36-month prospective freeze that starts July 2026.

Empirical Verdict

The paper tells the research path honestly through three models. The Sharpe Prototype found an attractive later window but fails a survival standard: its VIX-enabled cross-validation paths absorb at zero in March 2020, and it was selected after inspecting the window that flatters it. The Survival Allocator repairs the covariance, prices costs inside the objective, and makes the hard gates binding. The High Ceiling Allocator keeps those repairs, raises the risk ceiling to 25 percent, and is the one development path that remains credible after the licensed-quote audit.

The eight-name plus VIX book is the headline because its equity spread evidence is exact historical market-hours CBBO. The broader 56-name book leans on an inferred spread proxy, and the audit found real broad-name spreads wider than the proxy assumed: its touch-fill Sortino drops 17.5 percent against 9.8 percent for the headline book. Every reported path is retrospective development evidence, and confirmation requires 36 untouched monthly observations under frozen code, data rules, and hashes.

Research paper only. The results are historical backtests, not investment advice, live trading performance, or evidence that the book can scale beyond the displayed-depth capacity checks.
