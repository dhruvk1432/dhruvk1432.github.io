Dhruv Kohli · Admission-audit rewrite · June 2026

This paper is now framed as a capital-admission audit. It asks one question: which sleeves deserve risk budget after funding, transaction costs, option premia, cashflow accounting, drawdowns, and tail losses are charged?

The empirical work is unchanged, but the presentation is cleaner. The paper admits the downside-score cross-asset book as the main allocation, qualifies option sleeves as conditional downside protection, rejects the ML forecast arm as a capital rule, and keeps fixed option overlays as diagnostics rather than standing trades.

Admitted
Downside-score cross-asset allocation: 15.12% annual return, Sortino 2.00, max drawdown -19.87%.
Qualified
Option sleeves reduce drawdown and CVaR, but the return give-up keeps them conditional.
Rejected
ML forecasts remain research inputs because they do not beat the simpler rule at portfolio level.
Diagnostic
Fixed SPY put and collar overlays remain useful for stress checks, not capital admission.
